# Visual review and certification limits

A–F uses the existing project standard: A = convincingly location-specific; B = recognizable with material approximation; C = district/landmark language present but major approximation; D = weak resemblance; F = missing or incorrect. “NR” means not independently reviewed. Grades are visual judgments from the linked renderer evidence, not derived from passing technical checks. Existing inventory grades remain provisional unless explicitly reviewed here.

Could someone familiar with Bangkok recognize these environments without labels? **Chinatown: likely at district level**, through the gate, curved dense commerce and Chinese-Thai signage; not the identity of each real block. **Tha Tien: plausible in Wat Pho/river context**, but the low-rise buildings alone are not yet uniquely identifying. Neither benchmark is awarded A or presented as final citywide art completion.

Authentic foundations: district adjacency, riverbank, retained landmark relationships, curved Yaowarat direction, generic bilingual trades and researched low-rise heritage vocabulary. Deliberate compression: road widths/lengths, entrance-to-arrival connections, selected compound footprints and one representative canopy per MRT station. Approximate: individual shop parcels, hotel bearings, many secondary facade profiles, Tha Tien service courts and lighting balance.

The 138-record [inventory](../../../data/bangkok/iconic-inventory.v1.json) distinguishes present-but-inadequate, missing, excluded and research-required records. Name presence does not certify the intended mesh or orientation. Citywide screenshots are a repeatable survey, not blanket A-grade approval. Hotel reconstruction, exact business blocks, full transit station architecture and citywide landscape upgrades follow the [phased roadmap](roadmap.md).
