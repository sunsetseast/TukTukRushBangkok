## Palace–Tha Tien–Wat Pho riverfront

The real Palace western edge is not a river panorama park. Maha Rat, fortified walls, civic/riverside properties, Tha Chang landing, Tha Tien commercial blocks and narrow public ferry approaches occupy the space between the compounds and river. The research supports keeping compact commercial blocks and intermittent access. It does not support removing every roof between a water camera and a chedi.

The geographic river and road alignment from PR32 remains authoritative. The visible generic waterfront masses were generated by the ordinary base/frontage/infill systems and background facade simplification, not a misplaced river mesh. Changes address that specific band: low masonry commercial grammar, full facades even at background dispatch, rear shutters/doors and paved service edges. A low water-level camera can still see mostly commercial roofs and walls. This is retained in the comparison instead of raising the camera until the Palace looks unobstructed.

The two new reservations protect a public approach wedge and narrow alley, not a continuous promenade. Tha Chang uses the same pier projection and riverbank placement as other existing landings. The walk to Tha Tien remains pedestrian scale; no road or navigation edge was added. All footprint changes are confined to x (-1120,-820), z (-1120,-520). The map displays actual world roads, river width and delta footprints; it is not a cadastral plan.

## Reading the tests

The original 24 collision-volume rays are retained and expanded to 37 with main gates, side streets, normal moving approaches, perpendicular intersections, river and elevated observations. Roof/compound visibility, entrance visibility and principal-feature visibility are separate labels. Foreground hits are not automatically errors; no-target hits are not successes. The five retained cases are plausible dense urban occlusions with uncertain exact property correspondence, not proven authentic building-by-building obstructions.
