## Baseline defects verified in the source

- Giant Swing shared a 31m slab with a tiny temple hall. The red frame was useful, but the compound was compressed into the same plot and did not communicate the true temple/square relationship.
- Pak Khlong Talat used six identical small sheds with three low-poly flower cones on each counter. This read as a generic village market rather than Bangkok's wholesale district.
- Jim Thompson House had four heavily overlapping house blocks, one terrace and two trees. It omitted most of the compound, entry boundary, garden and canal setting.
- River City used one 70m slab, an isolated glazed arch and small terrace blocks. Direct inspection of the VCON river-facing project photograph showed that this arch does not describe the present facade. The first draft of this PR inherited that error; it was replaced before final QA with the documented dark screened facade. Its riverside public frontage and rear arrival were also poorly distinguished.

## Implemented geometry and access contract

`historicDistricts.ts` exports `HISTORIC_SITE_PLANS` so placement and collision use separate building masses, not a single property-sized obstacle. The root world integration authors exact reservations, street stops and local roads. Roof overhangs and tree crowns may extend up to 2m beyond the plan and require a visual reservation margin.

- **Swing / Suthat:** 86 × 118m compound, paved northern civic forecourt and swing, separate wihan and ordination hall to the south, side and rear cloisters, white boundary, gate gap, benches and lamps. The temple compound is pedestrian space; the fare stop belongs on the surrounding public street. The compressed square is not presented as a copy of the full real civic plaza.
- **Flower market:** 104 × 78m reserved district, two separately colliding covered market blocks, a clear central pedestrian/loading alley, heavily stocked awnings and a distinct Yodpiman riverfront arcade. Public tuk-tuk access stays on perimeter streets; flower carts do not occupy those roads. Flower heads, pots, chains and furniture are baked into three render batches per LOD before optional text signs.
- **Jim Thompson:** six separate raised house masses within a 68 × 64m walled garden, restrained domestic gables rather than gold temple finials, linked open terraces, mature trees, pond, south-facing gate and approach. The drop-off is outside the compound; the internal stairs and terraces are not claimed as vehicle routes.
- **River City:** 98 × 68m plot with four-storey arts-centre body, current dark screened river facade and staggered metallic banding, distinct low restaurant pavilions and river terrace, railings and seats, rear entry canopy. Vehicle drop-off belongs behind the mall; the water-facing terrace stays pedestrian space.

## Fidelity limits to retain in the final review

These models improve compound structure and street-facing identity. They do not reproduce every mural, window bay, tenancy or historic ornament. Market hall arrangement and museum house placement are stylized compressions. River City's facade and entry need screenshot comparison from both sides; its low triangle count is not evidence of visual approval. Exact neighborhood density depends on world-level infill and must be judged in the final rendered gallery. No A/B grade is assigned here before reviewing actual in-game images.
